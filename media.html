Accessibility and structure

Accessibility here is not a layer on top — it is how the components are built.

Video never travels alone

Every video carries its transcript on the same page. Captions and transcripts came from the video platform’s transcription workflow once it had been tested and adopted — one pipeline, every video, no separate provider.

Placeholder video · 03:45

Transcript. A transcript is a reading experience, not a compliance artefact — full sentences, speaker labels when they matter, and the same typography as the rest of the page.

Images declare their purpose

The accordion, rebuilt on native HTML

Accordions hold long, parallel detail — never required reading. This one has a story: the original was JavaScript-dependent, and a platform update quietly broke it. It was rebuilt on native HTML, so expanding and collapsing now belongs to the browser — keyboard support included, nothing left to break:

Take time out

Each accordion opens with a lead-in sentence on the page, so the hidden content is optional depth — a learner who never opens it misses detail, not meaning.

Breathe deeply

Headings in a set share one grammatical shape — all verb-first here — and stay short enough to hold one line.

Carry on reading

Between three and ten lines per panel. Less belongs in the paragraph above; more deserves its own page.

Structure you can feel from a keyboard

Tab from the top of this page: a skip link appears first, the focus ring is thick and indigo, and every interactive element is a real button or link. Heading order is strict, so rotor navigation reads like a table of contents.
