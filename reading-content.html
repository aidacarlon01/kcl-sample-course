Starting point and standard

Most of a course is reading. The standard makes reading effortless, so attention goes to the ideas.

Every page follows the same contract: one page title, headings in strict order, body text at 16px with a line length that stays comfortable, and components used for their purpose rather than decoration. This page demonstrates the reading set.

Headings carry the structure

An H2 marks each major section and carries the short keyline you can see above — a quiet visual anchor that also keeps skimming honest. H3 and H4 subdivide without ever skipping a level, because screen reader users navigate by heading order, not by appearance.

Lists do the sorting

Use a list when items are parallel; use prose when they are not. For example, a page in this standard needs:

  • one H1, supplied by the page banner;
  • headings in order, never chosen for their font size;
  • a purpose for every component — if the copy works as a paragraph, it stays a paragraph.

Wells hold asides

A well is for content that supports the page without belonging to its argument — context, reminders, practicalities:

Wells use the quietest grey in the palette so they sit beside the text, not on top of it. Anything urgent belongs in a note on the home page pattern instead.
